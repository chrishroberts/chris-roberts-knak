Where I fit

I have sold the platforms that sit next to Knak for twelve years, led the integrators and delivery teams on virtually every deal, and coached sellers inside the same methodologies. I am well versed in Command of the Message, which Salesforce deploys through Force Management, and I was trained on MEDDPICC and Challenger through Salesforce.

I also build account-based and outbound pipeline alongside marketing. I was the sole field seller selected to co-architect Salesforce’s first global HLS account-based marketing go-to-market model, and every prospecting touch I make follows the Filling the Funnel method.

How I run a sales team

A Revenue Operating System: methodology sets the language, coaching changes behavior, and competitive plays decide where to attack. One weekly cadence, measured on pipeline and win rate.

Methodology sets the language

MEDDPICC for deal inspection. Command of the Message for value stories. Sandler inside a buyer-controlled motion. Filling the Funnel for prospecting.

Coaching changes behavior

Call reviews and deal inspection built on the same vocabulary, so feedback is specific and repeatable across all three directors.

Competitive plays decide the attack

Named plays against Stensul, Beefree, Stripo and the editors built into marketing automation platforms, each tied to a buyer role and a proof point.

An architecture lens, kept vendor-neutral

Salesforce publishes a four-layer agentic enterprise architecture: System of Context, Work, Agency and Engagement, with a Trust spine across all four. I claim fluency and an operating-model view, not authorship. My read is that Knak sits where marketers do their work, MCP makes it callable from the agent layer, and brand governance is the Trust spine that large enterprises will buy on. Layers are durable and vendors within a layer are swappable, which is how I would sell it.

How I would approach the first 90 days

Days 1 to 30: listen

  • Ride along on live calls with each director.
  • Review open pipeline by source (marketing, rep and partner) and the last two quarters of losses.
  • Map the buying committee in the top accounts.

Days 31 to 60: standardize

  • One deal-inspection format across the team.
  • Value stories built on Knak customer proof.
  • A weekly pipeline and forecast cadence.
  • Joint account lists, personas and plays with Marketing and Sales Development for priority Fortune 100 accounts.

Days 61 to 90: multi-thread

  • Executive plays into brand, marketing operations, IT and legal.
  • Competitive plays by buyer role.
  • Coaching plan per director, tied to win rate.

This is an approach to test against what the team already does, not a finished plan.
